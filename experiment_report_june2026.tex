\section{Experiment 1: Interleaved Training v3}

\subsection*{Design}

Following your suggestion, this experiment revises the previous interleaved design (v2)
on two dimensions:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Episode-level game switching.} In v2, the active game switched every
        1\,000 environment steps, cutting episodes mid-way. In v3, the game only switches
        at a natural episode boundary. This avoids the truncation artefact.

  \item \textbf{Single joint replay buffer.} In v2, Pong and Breakout each had their own
        replay buffer sampled independently. In v3, all transitions from both games are
        stored in one shared buffer, tagged with a \texttt{game\_id}. When a training
        batch is sampled, Pong transitions are routed through the Pong head and Breakout
        transitions through the Breakout head; the loss from both subsets is summed before
        a single backward pass. This means the shared backbone (conv\,+\,fc\_repr) receives
        gradients from both games simultaneously.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Architecture:} \texttt{AtariCNNTwoHead} --- shared conv\,+\,fc\_repr (512-dim),
with separate output heads: \texttt{fc\_out\_pong} ($\rightarrow$6 actions) and
\texttt{fc\_out\_breakout} ($\rightarrow$4 actions). Trained from scratch (no Pong checkpoint).

\textbf{Budget:} 1\,M environment steps per game = 2\,M total. Learning rate $10^{-4}$,
buffer capacity 100\,k, batch size 32. Game selection at each episode boundary is
step-balanced: always switch to whichever game has accumulated fewer steps so far.

\subsection*{Results}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Steps} & \textbf{Pong reward} & \textbf{Breakout reward} & \textbf{Dead neurons} & \textbf{CKA drift} \\
\midrule
0             & $0.0$  & $0.0$ & 47.1\% & 0.010 \\
500\,k        & $-10.6$ & $4.1$ & 37.1\% & 0.040 \\
1\,M          & $-7.6$ & $3.5$ & 36.5\% & 0.047 \\
1.5\,M        & $-7.2$ & $3.2$ & 50.4\% & 0.045 \\
\textbf{2\,M (final)} & $\mathbf{-6.8}$ & $\mathbf{3.2}$ & \textbf{62.1\%} & \textbf{0.039} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Interleaved v3 metrics logged every 100\,k total steps. CKA measures similarity
to the original Pong DQN backbone (higher = more similar).}
\end{table}

\subsection*{Interpretation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Pong is meaningfully retained.} Final Pong reward of $-6.8$ sits between
        the freeze-conv-only baseline ($-14.0$) and the freeze-all baseline ($+7.2$).
        The shared backbone learns to serve both tasks without complete forgetting,
        despite no explicit regularisation or freezing.

  \item \textbf{Breakout learns normally.} Final Breakout reward of $3.2$ matches the
        standalone Breakout DQN baseline ($3.86$), showing joint training does not impair
        new game acquisition.

  \item \textbf{Comparison with v2.} The previous step-level design (v2) resulted in
        Pong collapsing to $-21$ and Breakout stalling at $0.4$ --- both tasks failed.
        The episode-level joint buffer design resolves both failures. This strongly
        validates the redesign you suggested.

  \item \textbf{Dead neurons increase late in training.} Dead neuron fraction drops from
        47\% initially to $\sim$37\% at 1\,M steps (both games partially active), then
        rises to 62\% by 2\,M. This late-phase increase may reflect the backbone
        over-specialising as both games individually approach convergence. The level
        remains well below the sequential no-freeze condition (87\%).

  \item \textbf{CKA drift is small and stable.} The backbone representation stays close
        to the original Pong reference throughout. Note these networks were trained from
        scratch, not initialised from the Pong checkpoint, so low CKA drift means both
        training regimes converge to similar representational geometry.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experiment 2: Sequential Transfer with Dead Neuron Reinitialisation}

\subsection*{Design}

Following the sequential training discussion: after training Pong for 2\,M steps,
identify dead neurons in fc\_repr and reinitialise them before starting Breakout training.

\textbf{Dead neuron criterion:} A neuron is declared dead if its post-ReLU activation
is zero in $>$95\% of 1\,000 sampled Pong frames.

\textbf{Reinitialisation:} The weight rows of dead neurons in fc\_repr are reset using
orthogonal initialisation (gain $= \sqrt{2}$), biases to zero --- the same scheme used
at network creation. The Adam optimiser is also recreated to clear stale momentum
associated with the reinitialised weights.

\textbf{Result of reinit:} 388 out of 512 neurons (75.8\%) were dead after Pong training.
All 388 were reinitialised.

\textbf{Budget:} Breakout trained for 2\,M steps under identical conditions to the
no-freeze sequential baseline. Pong retention measured every 200\,k steps via chimera
evaluation (current backbone + original frozen Pong head).

\subsection*{Results}

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\toprule
\textbf{Breakout steps} & \textbf{Pong reward (chimera)} & \textbf{Breakout reward} & \textbf{Dead neurons} & \textbf{CKA drift} \\
\midrule
0 (post-reinit, pre-training) & $-20.9$ & $0.0$  & 33.0\% & 0.065 \\
200\,k                        & $-21.0$ & $2.1$  & 47.5\% & 0.018 \\
600\,k                        & $-21.0$ & $3.9$  & 57.8\% & 0.031 \\
1\,M                          & $-21.0$ & $3.4$  & 63.1\% & 0.018 \\
1.6\,M                        & $-21.0$ & $5.2$  & 71.9\% & 0.028 \\
\textbf{2\,M (final)}         & $\mathbf{-21.0}$ & $\mathbf{3.5}$ & \textbf{77.5\%} & \textbf{0.025} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Sequential reinit metrics. Pong reward is the chimera evaluation: current
backbone weights combined with the original frozen Pong output head.}
\end{table}

\subsection*{Interpretation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Reinitialisation did not prevent forgetting.} Pong reward is at floor
        ($-21$) immediately after the reinit step (step 0), before any Breakout training
        has occurred. This is because reinitialising fc\_repr breaks the backbone's
        ability to produce the representations that the Pong head was trained to use,
        even though the Pong head weights are intact. The forgetting is structural, not
        a consequence of Breakout overwriting features --- it is caused by the reinit
        itself.

  \item \textbf{Dead neurons grow back rapidly.} After reinit, the dead neuron fraction
        drops to 33\% (from 76\% pre-reinit). But by 2\,M Breakout steps, it has climbed
        back to 77.5\% --- \emph{higher} than where Pong left it. Breakout training
        drives even more neurons to zero than Pong did. This suggests that dead neuron
        accumulation is not a pathology specific to Pong, but a general property of
        deep RL training with ReLU networks.

  \item \textbf{Breakout learns normally.} Breakout reward reaches 3.5 at 2\,M steps,
        comparable to the no-freeze sequential baseline and the standalone DQN, suggesting
        the reinit does not accelerate or impair Breakout acquisition.

  \item \textbf{Connection to continual backpropagation.} The result illustrates why
        one-shot reinitialisation at the task boundary is insufficient: it destroys
        existing useful representations without providing a mechanism for preventing
        their re-collapse during the new task. Continual backpropagation (Elsayed \&
        Mahmood 2024; Dohare et al.\ 2024) addresses this by continuously maintaining
        neuron utility throughout training, rather than intervening once.
\end{itemize}

\section{Open Questions for Next Meeting}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Terminology:} You referred to lower layers as ``representation layers''
        and higher layers as ``strategy/policy layers.'' Our codebase uses fc\_repr as
        the ``representation layer.'' Should we standardise to your framing before
        revising the report?

  \item \textbf{``Predate, make consistent'':} Could you clarify what you meant by this?
        We interpreted it as updating the report for consistency but are unsure of the
        exact scope.

  \item \textbf{CKA probe set:} For cross-game CKA, on-policy frames from one agent
        are off-distribution for the other. We used random frames as a neutral probe set.
        Is this justification acceptable, or do you have a preferred approach?

  \item \textbf{Page 16 / convergence:} Were you referring to the backbone experiment
        (5\,M steps) or the freeze-all sequential condition? Should we re-run to 10\,M
        steps or add a caveat?
\end{enumerate}
